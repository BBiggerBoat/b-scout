# B-Atlas Production Validation Batch 08 — Helmsman + Great Harbour + Greenline + North Pacific + Nimbus + FJORD + Sea Sport

**Release:** v6.38.0  
**Canonical architecture:** v1.4  
**Scope:** 19 current B-Atlas records.

## Helmsman

The 31 Sedan is explicitly described by Helmsman as an update of the Camano 31 design. B-Atlas records this as lineage rather than merging the two brands.

The 38E, 43E and 46 receive manufacturer-backed dimensions, semi-displacement/full-keel structure and running-gear protection. The 43E and 46 are corrected away from legacy aft-cabin assumptions.

## Great Harbour

GH37 and N37 remain separate generations. Great Harbour's own brochure describes the N-series as its second generation of full-displacement trawler yachts.

Both use very shallow-draft, wide-beam full-displacement hulls with twin low-output diesels. Their extreme beam, low center of gravity and shallow draft are deliberate design characteristics rather than bad data.

## Greenline

The Greenline 33 and 39 are important because a conventional `FuelCode` is insufficient.

These models were marketed in diesel, hybrid and electric configurations. B-Atlas therefore canonicalizes the hull/dimensional facts while leaving energy source configuration-specific.

## North Pacific

The 38 Sedan receives strong contemporary specifications from a 2013 Canadian Yachting review.

The 28 Pilothouse remains in B-Atlas with conservative canonical values because the older model-specific factory page is not currently available. Missing documentation reduces confidence; it does not eliminate the model.

## Nimbus

The current 365 Coupé receives manufacturer metric specifications. The model supports multiple Volvo configurations including both single and twin engines, so engine count is not forced model-wide.

The historic 3003 remains intentionally conservative until stronger primary model documentation is located.

## FJORD

The FJORD 36 and 40 families are deep-V/planing performance cruisers, not displacement boats.

The 36 Open and 40 Open are normalized around their documented IPS-powered configurations. Cruiser and Open identities remain separate because their deckhouse/layout concepts are materially distinct.

## Sea Sport

The XL 2400 and Explorer 2400 receive current factory specifications. Both are 8'6"-beam deep-V outboard pilothouse boats that can use single or twin engines.

The current B-Atlas `Sea Sport 27 — Seamaster / Navigator / Pilot` record is too broad. Rather than inventing one set of universal specifications, it is flagged for a later model-identity split review.

## Architecture result

This batch reinforces:
- design-lineage links without brand merging;
- second-generation model families;
- multi-energy propulsion;
- historical records with intentionally lower confidence;
- and the need to split combined model identities when marketing names represent materially distinct layouts.
